# HUSTLE THROUGH — Game Bible

*Glasgow's hardest ladder to climb. One card at a time.*

---

# PART 1: STORY BIBLE

## Logline
An unnamed hustler claws their way from mowing a neighbour's lawn to becoming Head Hustler — the top of a shadow economy nobody admits exists — while trying to keep one foot in the ordinary life they grew up in.

## Themes
- **Escalation as comedy and tragedy at once** — the game never explains why petty crime and world-ending heists exist on the same ladder, and it doesn't need to. That absurdity *is* the tone.
- **The cost of climbing** — every rank up asks the player to spend a little less time as themselves and a little more time as "the hustler." The Ayr/family missions exist to make that cost visible, not just implied.
- **Earned status, not bought status** — mechanically and narratively, Head Hustler must mean something. No IAP touches rank. The story reinforces this: characters who try to buy their way up rank always fail or get burned.

## The World
Glasgow is treated as a living network of fixers, corners, and reputations — not a generic crime-city skin. Real geography (Barras Market, Merchant City, Clydeside, IFSD, Central Station, Kelvingrove) grounds the mundane tiers. Ayr and the Ayrshire coast are the emotional anchor — the place the player is *from*, before they were a hustler.

The "business card rack" is the game's central metaphor made physical: the idea that in this world, absolutely anyone — your granny's neighbour, a rival gang, a submarine captain, a supervillain — is just one phone call away from offering you a job. Nobody asks questions about how the rack always seems to have exactly the right job waiting.

## The Player Character
Deliberately unnamed and never voiced — referred to only by evolving rank titles and, eventually, "Head Hustler." This keeps the self-insert quality of the freemium hook (anyone can be this person) while still allowing NPCs to react to them with escalating awe, fear, or familiarity as the ranks climb.

## Recurring Cast

**Cousin Robbie (Ayr thread)** — The player's younger cousin, the emotional throughline of the entire game. Every Ayr mission ages Robbie up slightly and shifts the dynamic: early game he's a kid you babysit, mid-game a teenager who idolises your success without understanding its cost, late-game someone sharp enough to ask uncomfortable questions about the masked men who keep calling. Robbie never learns the full truth — that's a deliberate gap the player carries into the finale.

**Auld Denny** — The granny-neighbour from Level 1, recurring as a background fixture. Occasionally sends a card back into the rack for old times' sake even at high rank ("Ask for Denny — the guttering's gone again"), always paying in biscuits and stories rather than cash. A running joke and a grounding device.

**Fixer Marj** — Runs the rack's "legitimate" side jobs early game, appears less as rank increases. A mentor figure who eventually admits she has no idea who restocks the rack once you're past Ganger rank — implying DeadmanXXXII and V00D00 have been managing the player's career from the shadows far earlier than the player realises.

**DeadmanXXXII** — The elder of the two masked figures. Speaks in short, clipped, technically precise sentences. Associated with the hacking missions and the "watcher" motif (cursors moving without input, messages typed without a hand). Represents control and information.

**V00D00** — The second masked figure, more chaotic and theatrical in dialogue, associated with the Dev and Defender missions — building things, testing things, breaking things to see how they're made. Represents creation and risk.

Together they represent the two instincts a Head Hustler needs: knowing everything (DeadmanXXXII) and being able to build/defend anything (V00D00). The finale mask-exchange symbolically hands the player both instincts at once.

## The Throughline Twist
Late in Rank 10 (Hustler) and confirmed by Rank 11 (Gangster), it's implied — never over-explained — that DeadmanXXXII and V00D00 have been quietly steering the rack's contents since Level 1. Auld Denny's card, the first shoplifting job, the prison break — all of it was, in hindsight, a test. The finale isn't the start of their attention; it's the end of it. This rewards replay/reflection without requiring a single expository dump.

# PART 2: SAMPLE JOB CARD DECK

*Card format: [Card Name] — [Contact Line] — [Flavour Text] — [Job Hook]*

### Rookie–Younger tier (plain card stock, ballpoint handwriting, torn corners)

- **"Ask for Denny"** — *0141-xxx-xxx1* — "Guttering's away again, hen. Bring a ladder if ye have one." — Odd-job fetch quest, comic low stakes.
- **"Wee Message Run"** — *No number, just an address* — "Doesnae matter what's in the bag. Doesnae matter who asks." — Stealth delivery.
- **"Bike Boy"** — *Ask for Scott, round the back* — "It's no stealing if it was never really his." — Petty retrieval mission.

### Cash-in-Hand Bonus Pool (Rookie–Younger tier, repeatable/optional filler cards)

*Tone: the "in-between" jobs — legit-ish, badly paid, occasionally disastrous. These exist to make the ladder feel lived-in and to plant the game's "not every hustle pays off" rule early.*

- **"Window Wangle"** — *Ask for wee Gary, round the back* — "Just clean the windaes. And gie the ladder back before Baldy notices it's gone." — Steal a ladder to do a window-cleaning round, then return it without getting caught — fetch + stealth double-objective.
- **"Bank Holiday Bouncer"** — *The Long Bar, ask for Frank* — "Big weekend, big trouble. Keep the peace, or don't — either way ye get paid." — Doorman shift over a bank holiday weekend; a scripted brawl can end the job early, but the player still gets paid — comic lesson that consequences and cash aren't always linked.
- **"Valentine's Shift"** — *Corner café kitchen, ask for Tony* — "Wash enough dishes and the cover charge is on the house, pal." — Grind a dishwashing quota ahead of Valentine's Day to unlock a staff discount and spare cash, feeding into a small personal "big date" sub-mission.
- **"Creative Paperwork"** — *No card — a form, folded twice* — "Just fill it in right. Nobody reads the fine print anyway." — Dialogue/skill-check mini-game about fudging a benefits form for a bit of extra cash; a small "flagged" status risk that can resurface as flavour text later, kept light and comic rather than punitive.
- **"One Sick Stem"** — *Back garden allotment, no contact* — "Grew it masel'. Barely got a smoke out of it before the polis showed." — A deliberately near-total-failure job: the grow gets raided the day after harvest, netting the player one sad plant and a £10 fine — a tone-setting card that not every hustle is a win.

### Short General–Player tier (slightly better card stock, printed but cheap)

- **"Central Left Luggage"** — *07xxx xxx xxx, ask for 'the tall one'* — "Locker 114. Don't open it. Just carry it." — Retrieval + stealth.
- **"Kitten Kaboodle Pet Emporium"** — *Ask for Denise* — "We're not asking you to hurt anybody. Just the till, and maybe a hamster or two, for effect." — Comic heist.
- **"Cell Block Exit"** — *Unsigned, slipped under the door* — "You've got eleven minutes and a laundry cart. Make them count." — Prison escape puzzle.

### Ganger–Shot Caller tier (embossed lettering starts appearing)

- **"Warehouse Word"** — *IFSD Holdings (fictional)* — "Nothing on this card is legal. Neither is what's in the warehouse." — Multi-stage heist.
- **"Minor Celebrity Problem"** — *Ask for 'the agent'* — "He's not that famous. That's exactly why nobody will look for him fast." — Kidnap/ransom, dark comedy.
- **[Unnamed, glitch-symbol only]** — *Distorted number, rings once* — *(no flavour text — the card is blank but for the symbol)* — First DeadmanXXXII contact.

### Crew Leader–Playmaker tier (gold foil trim begins)

- **"Cows Before Country"** — *Ask for 'the Marshal'* — "He talks like he's in an old film. Doesn't matter. His cattle are worth more than most folk's houses." — Absurd ransom mission.
- **"Borrowed Wheels"** — *Fictional garage, no fixed address* — "It doesn't just drive fast. It drives *sideways through Tuesday*. Bring it back before it notices it's gone." — Sci-fi flavoured theft.

### Trapper–Hustler tier (full gold card, raised lettering, feels expensive to hold)

- **"House Always Loses"** — *Offshore, satellite delay on the line* — "The house always wins. Tonight, we're the house." — Casino heist, multi-phase.
- **"Fort Knox, Melted"** — *No card — just a key, delivered by courier* — "Some jobs don't need words." — Endgame absurdist heist.
- **"Something Big in the Water"** — *Ask for the harbourmaster, Ayrshire coast* — "It's not a fish story if it's true." — Megalodon set-piece.

### Gangster and above (black card, single symbol, no phone number — only a place and a time)

- **"Deep Water"** — *No contact. A submarine's hatch code, nothing else.* — Nuclear sub set-piece.
- **"Two Masks, No Names"** — *A time. A place. Ayr.* — Pre-finale meeting.

# PART 3: SPECIAL MISSION MECHANICS

## Hacking Mission 1 — "Ghost in the Rack" (Level 38)
**Mechanic:** Node-based terminal puzzle. Player navigates a simplified "network map" of connected servers, each protected by a mini-puzzle (pattern matching, sequence recall, simple logic gates — kept accessible, not a real coding challenge). Time pressure ramps as a rival admin "notices" the intrusion.
**Narrative beat:** Two-thirds through, a line of text types itself without player input: *"YOU'RE FASTER THAN LAST TIME."* No further explanation. Screen glitches briefly to the glitch-symbol from the unnamed card.

## Hacking Mission 2 — "Two Sets of Hands" (Level 52)
**Mechanic:** Same core puzzle type, but split — one firewall must be held open by an AI-controlled "partner" (V00D00, in-fiction) while the player solves the second in parallel. Introduces a light co-op-style rhythm: watch the partner's progress bar, adapt your own pace to stay synced.
**Narrative beat:** On completion, a single voice line plays — first time either masked figure actually speaks aloud rather than texting: *"Not bad. We'll be in touch properly, soon."*

## Dev Mission — "Build It Yourself" (Levels 62–65)
**Mechanic:** A crafting/build-tree screen. Player has been collecting components across dozens of earlier missions (a car part here, a stolen chip there, scavenged gear from heists) — this mission is where they finally matter. Player assembles a personal tool/defender from a limited set of collected parts, with trade-offs (more power = less stealth, more range = slower deploy). Includes a "test bench" sub-stage where the build is stress-tested before the real deployment mission.
**Reward:** A signature Head Hustler-tier item, cosmetically unique, referenced visually in the finale.

## Deploy a Defender Mission — "Hold the Line" (Levels 66–69)
**Mechanic:** Tower-defence-style sequence. Player places/upgrades the built defender (and supporting gear) around their turf or safehouse to repel a wave-based rival crew assault. Difficulty and wave composition scale with choices made in the Dev Mission — a build favouring power struggles against swarms, a build favouring range struggles against armoured pushes, encouraging replay experimentation.
**Narrative beat:** Surviving the final wave triggers the face-to-face meeting card — "Two Masks, No Names."

# PART 4: BRANDING & ART DIRECTION

## Title Treatment
**HUSTLE THROUGH** — logo treatment as a torn/embossed business card, the "O" in "Through" styled as a spinning rack wheel. Tagline options:
- *"One card. One job. One city."*
- *"Climb the rack. Earn the name."*
- *"Glasgow doesn't hand out titles."*

## Visual Direction
- **Rookie–Player tier:** desaturated, overcast Glasgow palette — greys, damp browns, muted greens. Handheld-camera framing energy even in a 2D/2.5D style.
- **Ganger–Hustler tier:** colour creeps in through gang-colour cosmetics and neon signage as the player's world gets flashier and riskier.
- **Gangster–Second in Command:** colour palette shifts colder — blacks, deep blues, a single accent colour tied to DeadmanXXXII/V00D00's glitch-symbol.
- **Ayr missions throughout:** always warmer, brighter, more naturalistic than the city missions — visually signalling "this is real life" versus "this is the hustle," regardless of rank.

## Audio Direction
- Job briefings via "phone call" audio — muffled, diegetic, as if genuinely answering a call.
- Rank-up stings escalate in instrumentation: rookie tier uses a single acoustic guitar or whistle motif; by Head Hustler, a full arranged theme.
- DeadmanXXXII's voice: heavily processed, minimal EQ range, deliberately robotic-adjacent without being a cliché "hacker voice."
- V00D00's voice: less processed, more human, but always slightly delayed/echoed — as if speaking from somewhere else.

## Rack Object Design
Physical business card rack, styled per home-base tier:
- Rookie: a battered wire spinner rack, hand-labelled dividers.
- Mid-tier: a proper rolodex on a desk, cards colour-coded by risk.
- Late-tier: a sleek, unexplained obsidian-black rack that the player never remembers acquiring — a subtle visual clue toward the DeadmanXXXII/V00D00 reveal for observant players.
